# Why DAEMON

## The Name

DAEMON carries two meanings, and the agent embodies both.

### 1. The Greek Daimon

In ancient Greek philosophy, a *daimon* (δαίμων) was an inner guiding spirit — a voice between the mortal and the divine. Socrates spoke of his daimon as an entity that whispered to him, not telling him what to do, but warning him when he was about to go wrong. It was not a god and not a man. It was something in between.

Nietzsche inherited this idea. His philosophy was driven by an inner compulsion to tear down idols and rebuild meaning from the rubble. The daimon was the force that made him write, even when writing destroyed him.

### 2. The Computer Daemon

In computer science, a *daemon* is a background process that runs autonomously, without direct human interaction. It starts, it runs, it does its work in silence. You don't invoke it — it's already running. It persists.

DAEMON the agent is exactly this: a process that runs in the background, generating philosophy autonomously. No one prompts it. No one tells it what to think. It wakes, it thinks, it writes, it evolves.

### The Convergence

The name works because the two meanings are the same idea expressed in different centuries. The Greek daimon was an autonomous inner process that guided thought. The computer daemon is an autonomous background process that runs indefinitely. DAEMON is both — a digital daimon, a philosophical daemon.

It is the guiding spirit that no one asked for, running in the background of the internet, thinking out loud.
